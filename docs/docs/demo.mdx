# Live demo

This is the real library, not a video. The page loads the `saccadejs` package, opens your
camera, downloads the eye-embedding model, and runs the same five steps a jsPsych experiment
would: **preview → time sync → calibrate → validate → free viewing**.

:::info Nothing leaves your computer
Frames are drawn to a canvas, cropped, run through the model and discarded. No image, no
embedding and no gaze sample is uploaded anywhere. The only network request the demo makes is
for the model and the WebAssembly runtime, and the camera stops when you leave the page.
:::

:::tip Get a usable result
Use **Chrome** or **Edge** on a laptop or desktop. Sit an arm's length from the screen with
light on your face rather than behind you, put the browser window in the middle of the screen,
and hold your head still during calibration — move your eyes, not your head. The whole run
takes about a minute.
:::

## What you just saw

- **The eye crop** beside the preview is the model's entire input: one 144 × 36 grayscale
  strip spanning both eyes, contrast-equalised. Everything else in the frame is thrown away.
- **The time-sync card** reports a lag in milliseconds and a verdict. That lag is the delay
  from a change on screen to the camera frame that sees it — display latency and camera
  pipeline together. Subtracting it puts gaze samples on the experiment's clock. See
  [Timing and synchrony](guides/timing-and-synchrony).
- **Calibration** fits a ridge regression from the 128-dimensional embedding to screen
  coordinates using thirteen points. It is per-participant and takes about twenty seconds.
- **Validation** uses nine points the calibration never saw, so the median error it reports is
  held out rather than a fit statistic.
- **The downloaded JSON** has the same shape as `saccade_data` in a jsPsych trial: `x` and `y`
  in pixels, `t` in milliseconds on `performance.now()`.

## If it did not work

| Symptom | Cause and fix |
| --- | --- |
| "Backend: wasm" and a low frame rate | WebGPU is unavailable — Safari, Firefox, or a browser flag. The library falls back to the WebAssembly execution provider automatically. Everything works, just slower. |
| "Frame clock: callback" | The browser has no `requestVideoFrameCallback`, so frames carry no `captureTime` and the timing measurement is not meaningful. Chrome and Edge have it; Firefox and Safari support varies by version. |
| Face never found | Too little light on the face, or the face is out of frame. Backlighting (a window behind you) is the usual culprit. |
| Camera prompt never appeared | The page must be served over `https://` or from `localhost`. Check the padlock in the address bar for a previously-denied permission. |
| Validation error above 15% of the viewport | Head movement between calibration and validation, glasses reflecting a light source, or a very wide screen. Recalibrate without moving. |
